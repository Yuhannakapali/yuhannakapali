Some films argue. This one simply pays attention. We follow Hirayama through the same days, the same cassette tapes, the same trees photographed through the same lens, and slowly the repetition stops feeling like a trap and starts feeling like a practice.

## What stayed with me

The film trusts silence. It lets a face do the work that most scripts hand to dialogue, and it believes that a small life, fully lived, is worth two hours of our attention. I left it wanting to notice more.

It is not a perfect film. The final act leans on a reveal it does not quite need. But the craft is so patient that I forgave it easily.
